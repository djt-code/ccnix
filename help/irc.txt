irc: an IRC client: BitchX's looks, weechat's buffers, irssi's scripts

  irc [network | host[:port] [nick]]

A full-screen IRC client.  With no arguments it starts with the networks marked autoconnect; given a network's name (/network add) or a host, it connects there.  Any IRC server: an ircd on another computer here (bin/ircd: NickServ, ChanServ, and #minecraft, the game's chat), or a real one through the ccnix mod's gateway, when the network reaches out (6667: no TLS here).

The screen: a title bar (the channel's topic), the chat with each nick in its own colour, the status bar ([time] [nick] [buffer] [Act: the buffers with something new] [Lag]) and the line you type.  On a wide screen (a monitor, 80 columns) the buffer list is on the left and the channel's nicks on the right.  Each server, channel and private conversation is a buffer, numbered: Alt+1..9 (or Esc then the digit) go to one, Alt+Left/Right or Ctrl+P/N step through them, Alt+A goes to the next with something new, PgUp/PgDn and the wheel scroll, Tab completes nicks, channels and commands.  The line edits as ccsh's does (lib/readline: Ctrl+A/E, Ctrl+K/U/W/Y, Alt+B/F, Up and Down, Ctrl+R). Ctrl+C then b, c (and colour digits), u, r, i or o puts mIRC's bold, colour, underline, reverse, italic or plain into the line (shown there as a reversed letter: B, C ...).  CC's font has no bold, underline or italic: the chat's text is light grey and bold shows white.  Ctrl+T quits.  Your nick (or hilight_words) in a channel, or a private message, chimes on a speaker if one is attached.

Commands (/help lists them all; /help <command> says more):
  /connect <network | host> [port] [nick]   /disconnect   /reconnect   /quit [message]
  /network add <name> <host> [port]         /network list   /network del <name>
  /join #chan [key]   /part [msg]   /msg nick text   /query nick   /me text
  /notice   /nick   /topic   /names   /whois   /who   /list   /invite
  /mode   /op /deop /voice /devoice   /kick   /ban /unban /kb   /away /back
  /ctcp nick cmd   /ping nick   /quote <raw line>   /lastlog text   /clear
  /window <n | close | list | move n>   /close   /set [name [value]]
  /alias name expansion   /ignore mask [levels]   /script load name
Any other command goes to the server as it is (/stats, /admin...). Aliases come with it (/j /wc /q /w /t /k /m /n /ns /cs /mc ...): /alias lists them; $0 $1- $* $N (your nick) $C (the channel) in your own.

Settings (/set; kept in ~/.irc/config, ~ being $HOME or /):
  nick, alt_nick, user_name, real_name, quit_message, part_message,
  away_message, timestamp_format (%H:%M), timestamp_clock (real, or
  game: the world's clock), prefix_width (9; 0 for <nick> text),
  hilight_words, beep_on_hilight, scrollback (500), buflist and
  nicklist (on, off, auto), reconnect_time (30), autojoin_on_invite,
  show_banner, scripts (loaded at the start).
A network's: /set network.<name>.<key> with address, port, password, nick, user_name, autojoin ("#a, #b"), autoconnect (on), sasl_user and sasl_pass (SASL PLAIN), commands (run once connected, ; between).

  /network add lab 10.1.1.5
  /set network.lab.autojoin #minecraft, #ccnix
  /set network.lab.autoconnect on
  /set network.lab.commands msg NickServ IDENTIFY hunter2

Scripts, as irssi has them: Lua files in ~/.irc/scripts (and those that come with ccnix), loaded with /script load <name>; the ones in ~/.irc/scripts/autorun, and those named in the scripts setting, at the start.  A script gets the irc table: signal_add(name, fn) (irssi's signals: "message public", "message private", "message join", "print text", "event <command>", "send text"...), signal_stop(), command_bind(name, fn, help), settings_add_str/int/bool, settings_get_*, timeout_add(ms, fn), statusbar_item(name, fn), print, and the buffers and servers, with their own methods (buffer:print, server:command, server:send_raw).  Unloading takes it all away again. `help irc-scripts` has the whole API and the scripts that come with it (autoaway, autorejoin, hilightwin, slap, nickserv).
