gpsd: a GPS host: answers CC's GPS, where computers ask where they are

  gpsd
      serve (run it as a service: see below)

Any computer, turtle or pocket computer with a wireless modem finds its position with gps.locate() (or `gps locate`), from the GPS hosts in range: it pings, each host answers with its position, and the distances its modem measured to the answers fix where it is.  gpsd is such a host, as `gps host` is, but run by init: it starts at boot, starts again if it ends, and logs.  It answers on every wireless modem (ender modems too), and on modems attached later.

Where it is: gpsd.x, gpsd.y and gpsd.z (the computer's coordinates, F3 in the game).  Without them, a command computer knows its own; another locates itself from the other hosts (tried every 10 s until there's a fix; not saved: a computer moved locates again at boot).  A position from the settings is checked when it starts: it pings, and weighs each host's answer, the distance measured against the distance between the two positions.  If most of the hosts that answer disagree (two or more), the position is wrong, or the computer was moved, and gpsd doesn't serve: it says so in red on the console and checks again every minute.  A host that disagrees while most agree is named in the log: its position, or this one, is wrong.  gpsd.check=false skips the check.

A constellation: four hosts at least, not all at one height (four in one plane leave two answers), as far apart as the modems reach, high up (a wireless modem reaches farther the higher it is; an ender modem reaches the whole dimension).  Then more where the turtles work: a client needs four in range.  Pocket computers and turtles can't host: they move.

Settings: gpsd.x, gpsd.y, gpsd.z; gpsd.check (true: check the position at start); gpsd.facility (daemon: the system log's facility).

To keep it running in the background, with init (`help init`):
  service add gpsd gpsd
  service enable gpsd             (at boot)
  service start gpsd
deploy/setup gpsd does all that (`setup gpsd --at x,y,z` sets the position too).  It logs when it starts serving, what the check found, and every 10 minutes how many requests it has answered, if any: `service status gpsd` shows the last of it (`help syslog`).
