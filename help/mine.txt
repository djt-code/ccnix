mine: tunnel-mining turtle for CC:Tweaked

Needs a pickaxe (any *_pickaxe upgrade), equipped or in the inventory. Any other tool is parked in the inventory while it runs and put back afterwards.  Optional: Advanced Peripherals Chunky Turtle upgrade so it keeps running unattended.

Usage:  mine [<width> <height> [length]] [--spacing N] [--no-lights] [--here]
             [--origin x,y,z[,facing]]
        mine --resume
            keep digging the tunnel it stopped in (from the start, or from the last home)
        mine ... --new
            start a fresh tunnel, forgetting the old one
        mine haul
            shuttle the valuables in the homes back to the start, as many trips as it takes
        mine haul --supplies
            ...and the supplies in the last home too
        mine reset
            back to the last home it built, after a Ctrl+T or restart (to the start, when the tunnel is finished)
        mine reset --to-start
            back to the start instead
        mine reset --here
            turtle was put back at the start by hand
        mine where
            where it is along the tunnel: at which home, or between which two; the tunnel's homes (it reads the saved job and position, so it works while mine runs, as a service say)

--origin gives the world cell of the start (what F3 shows for the block the turtle stands in) and the compass way it faces, north, east, south or west (left out, it is worked out from a Geo Scanner scan), for a run from the start.  The map then knows where it is in the world (T.toWorld, T.toLocal), for claude and the other programs.  GPS overrides it, with a warning, where they disagree.

With a wireless modem on the turtle (or in its inventory: it's swapped in for the check) and GPS hosts in range, its saved position is checked against GPS when it starts and at `reset`: put right if it's up to 16 blocks off, found if it was lost; farther off (carried away, or another dimension) it stops and says where GPS puts it.  The first check sets the world origin, if there's none (`help turtlelib`; sysctl turtle.gps=false turns it off).

A tunnel saved before a re-zero that moved the start (another program started away from home, T.clearPos) is in the old frame: --resume, haul and reset refuse it, say why and mark it stale; a fresh tunnel replaces it.

width  = blocks to the RIGHT of the turtle (negative = to the LEFT)
height = blocks UP from the turtle (negative = DOWN)
length = blocks the tunnel runs ahead; leave it out to keep going until the turtle can't (the start's chest full, bedrock)

With no size, it digs the one in the settings: 3x3, 32 long unless changed (`sysctl mine.width=5`, `sysctl mine.length=0` for no end; also mine.height, mine.spacing, mine.lights).  A size given here is saved there, so the next `mine` (or the autostart) digs the same.

Asked for again from the same start (`mine 10 10 --new`, or the settings' tunnel), a tunnel of the same width and height is carried on from its last home, as `--resume` does, keeping its own spacing: the part dug already is gone over, not dug again.  A finished tunnel is carried on when the length asked for now is longer (or has no end).

It keeps going rather than stopping (each a setting):
 * A home's chest that fills grows into a double chest: a second chest put beside it joins it (only from chests to spare beyond the next home's two).  When a haul empties a grown valuables chest, its second chest is picked up again, so homes use as few chests as they can.
 * A home's valuables chest full with nothing to grow it: the turtle hauls every home's valuables to the start (as many trips as it takes), goes straight back out and carries on (`sysctl mine.haul=false` stops instead).  Only the start's chest being full stops it.  `sysctl mine.haulEvery=N` hauls after every N homes too.
 * A bulk chest full with nothing to grow it: what doesn't fit is thrown on the floor behind the home (it despawns), and the run goes on.
 * Out of chests for the next home, or of fuel: it goes back to the start (hauling on the way, and taking fuel from the homes it passes), says what it needs (and tells the player, with mine.report), and looks in the top chest every minute until it's there, then goes back out and on (`sysctl mine.wait=false` stops instead).
 * A finished tunnel brings its last home's supplies (chests, fuel, torches, wood, the crafting table) back to the start, for the next tunnel; one that stopped leaves them there for `--resume`.

A spawner in the way (a dungeon's) doesn't stop it either: it's left standing (never dug), lit with a torch beside each of its sides that's in the tunnel and one on top, and the tunnel goes round it.  So do a chest, barrel or shulker box in the way.  Only a 1x1 tunnel, with no way round, stops there.

Through open water (an ocean, a lake, an aquifer, a lava lake), where nearly every block is wet, it works another way: from the home, a chunk at a time, it puts a shell of bulk blocks round the tunnel first (on the floor, walls and ceiling where water or lava may touch them: a scan says, else what it has seen), then fills the water inside in and digs it out dry, the blocks coming back.  Meanwhile, when more than half of the tunnel ahead is water, a bulkhead across the tunnel behind the home keeps the tunnel behind it dry, and a segment that ends in water gets an end cap, which the next one starts from.  It needs bulk blocks for that (cobblestone, from the homes behind, then the start's bottom chest).  With a Geo Scanner, the scans say where that is and how much of it is water.  Without one, it counts the water it digs next to, by where it is across the tunnel (a source counts far more than flowing water), so that a few stray sources don't count for much; once there's enough of it close together, it digs the next few blocks of the tunnel's top row and sides first (they're dug anyway), to see how full of water the tunnel is there.  More than half, and it goes on in open water; less, and it tunnels on as ever, sealing the water as it meets it.

Water coming into the tunnel from outside it (falling through a hole in the ceiling, or running in through a wall) is followed out, the way it flows (up a falling column, then along to stronger water), to its source, which is filled, so it stops for good.  It goes only through the water (no digging), never into a protected area, and no more than 16 blocks out, and comes back the way it went.  Where that doesn't stop it (a body of water, more than one source) or can't be done, water falling in through the ceiling is caught right over the hole in a bowl of bulk blocks: four sides (and a rim on them, where it's open round them), the hole filled as its bottom.  The water lands in it and stays there (water never overflows: it only runs down).  Falling columns side by side get one trough round them all.  Water it can't trace that runs in through a wall, a source, and lava are sealed where they come in, as before.

The turtle's own column and row are part of the tunnel: it stands in the mouth at one bottom (or top, for negative heights) corner, and the tunnel starts with the block in front of it.  `mine 3 3 32` digs a 3x3 tunnel 32 long, up and to the right of the turtle.

Placement: put the turtle where the tunnel's corner should start, facing along it.  Put a chest directly ABOVE the start for valuables and one directly BELOW it for bulk (stone, cobblestone, deepslate, dirt, gravel, sand...).  Stock the top chest with fuel and with CHESTS: the turtle builds a new home out of two of them every `spacing` blocks. Torches in it too, and it lights the tunnel (unless --no-lights).  And a crafting table, so it can craft more chests and torches from wood it finds (see Crafting below).  And an empty bucket, so it burns the lava it meets for fuel (see Lava below).
