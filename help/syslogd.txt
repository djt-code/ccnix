syslogd: the system log daemon: files, shows and forwards what's logged

  syslogd
      run (as a service: setup init does it)

Programs log through lib/log (C's syslog(3)), shell scripts with `logger`, and init logs what each service prints; all of it comes here, and goes where /etc/syslog.conf says: a log file, the console, or another syslog server (forwarded as RFC 5424 over UDP).  `help syslog` has the whole of it.  The first time it runs it writes /etc/syslog.conf with the default rules, to edit; without it (deleted) they are:
  *.info;auth,authpriv.none;local6,local7.none
      /var/log/messages
  auth,authpriv.*
      /var/log/auth.log
  local6.*
      /var/log/telnetd.log
  local7.*
      /var/log/httpd.log
  *.crit
      /dev/console
(telnetd logs as local6 and httpd as local7 unless telnetd.facility or httpd.facility say otherwise.)  The rules are read when it starts: `service restart syslogd` after changing them.  What's logged while it isn't running waits in init (the last 200).

Settings:
  syslog.maxSize
      a log file past this many bytes is moved to <name>.1 and started again (32768; K or M after it for KiB or MiB: `set syslog.maxSize 2M` on a bigger disk)
  syslog.keep
      how many old files to keep: .1, .2... (1; 0: none)
  syslog.burst
      at most this many messages from one program in 10 s; past that they're dropped, and counted (100)
  syslog.listen
      true: take messages from other computers on UDP (RFC 5424 or RFC 3164), as a loghost (false)
  syslog.port
      the port it listens on (514)

It needs init (`help init`).  Forwarding and listening need the network (`ip up`); a name in an @host rule is looked up (net.hosts, DNS) aside, and messages wait for it (the last 50).
