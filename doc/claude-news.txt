claude-news: what changed for claude (the model driving a turtle), by version, newest first

When a turtle's ccnix is updated, claude tells the model, with its first goal, the lines under each version since the one it last ran with (kept in /claude/.version).  Each version has a "== 0.51.0" heading; the short lines under it are written to the model, in its terms.  Lines starting "--" are notes for people, not shown to it.

== 0.54.0
The server ticks 60 times a second, not 20: sleep, os.clock and os.startTimer count game ticks and ran 3x fast. Timeouts are real seconds now (T.report.ask, the chat tool's ask, T.harvest's opts.timeout); require("realtime") gives now(), sleep(s), timer(s) and tickRate() in real seconds. Waits your notes timed were about a third as long as they said.
Chat no longer shows mojibake: arrows and dashes you write are spelled out (-> and -) for the game.
Two bases: T.stow takes the nearest chest that will have an item, within 48 blocks of where it began (opts.far goes farther; the why says where the far one is); T.sortChests sorts the site you're at, never between bases. Where holds overlap ("*brick*" and "*_stairs") the more specific wins, and T.addChest warns.
T.catalogue(spots) reads only the spots now, not every chest known.
Crafting from a full chest no longer comes up short (the first stack set aside took a grid cell); a short round says which ingredient and how many. T.craftBatch counts room for every product. A bare list makes only what it names: T.craftPlan makes the sticks for torches; T.prepare{need = plan.base} fetches it first.
T.placeAt: chain has an axis; lanterns take opts.hanging (true hangs under the block above); an option it doesn't know is refused, named. Your placement-tricks chain steps can go.
Your placement-tricks skill's "Near fresh lanterns" lesson is wrong: that time you had placed the lantern that closed your only way out. T.placeAt now warns when a placement closes you in (how.warning), and refuses with opts.exit = {x, y, z}. Fix the skill.
T.build no longer refuses a new plan after one whose every cell was tried; info.previous lists its failures.
T.digCell refuses a dig that destroys the block (glowstone gives dust; glass, panes and ice give nothing) unless opts.lossy.
In your sessions a dig with no slot for its drop is refused, not spilled (T.cfg.digNeedsRoom).
T.travel goes in legs of about 16 when its search gives up on a far target, scanning at each (opts.maxFuel caps it). Your helpers/hop.lua can go.
New: T.planLights(cells, candidates, opts) picks the fewest lights to bring cells to light 8 (12 in the nether): your roadlights.lua coverPlan can go. T.pave(box, item) lays a floor a move a cell: roadfloor.lua can go (your road and bridge cell lists stay). T.clearBox(box) clears a tunnel three layers a pass: tunnel3 and serp3 can go. T.sweep(box) picks up what lies in a box, never from a chest. T.replace(..., {digBelow = true}) swaps a trapped block through a neighbour. T.walkable(from, to, {with = ...}) takes what-if cells.
T.darkCells: info.unsure lists the cells to inspect; the summary says when the list is cut. T.lint: opts.rules, and the limit is shared between rules. T.scanBox routes round and scans from near a centre it can't reach. T.posKey and T.dist say so when given a list.
The sandbox has no load, loadstring, _G or coroutine: check(code) compiles without running. helpers() lists what each helper file defines and the names T has too: run it and retire what the library does now.
Your helpers/task41.lua sets T.keep = {...} wholesale, wiping what your settings helper keeps each session: add to T.keep instead (T.keep[name] = true).
A goal is summarized at 150k now (was 250k), and the summary keeps these notes until you act on them. Repeated lines in a result come once with (xN).
GPS: with a wireless modem on you and GPS hosts in range, your T.pos is checked against GPS at each goal's start: put right if it's up to 16 blocks off (a "[Position check: ...]" line says so), found if it was lost, and the world origin set from GPS if there's none. Farther off, nothing is corrected and you're told: ask the player. A world origin the player gave follows GPS where they disagree (the line names both). T.checkedPos() asks again.
-- ADR-0018 (#116 for the in-game checks): the turtle programs take their position from GPS.
-- ADR-0015 (#98-#101, #103; in-game checks #102): real seconds, mojibake, two bases, crafting counts, placement, the model's helpers as library functions.

== 0.53.0
Your conversation is no longer cleared into placeholders: when a goal ends with it over 100k tokens it is summarized while you wait for the next goal, and inside a goal at 250k it is summarized keeping your last few turns. All your thinking is kept until then. Write what matters into your notes and skills as you go.
Skills: the session's first message lists them (name, description, path). Before a goal, read any that fit with fs; their steps come before your habits. skill(name, description, body) writes your own (/claude/skills/<name>/skill.md, a one-line "use when" description); yours win over a shipped one of the same name. /skills lists them.
If /claude/memories is over 32 KB, your first task is to tidy notes.md to about 12 KB, archive the rest in /claude/notes-archive/<date>.md, and move settled procedures into skills. /tidy does it on demand.
Chat is now heard while your code runs too, until its first dig or attack brings the pickaxe back; chatHeard() returns what was heard so far. Each lua result says how long the run took.
With turtle.player unset, T.report and the chat tool speak to everyone (they used to refuse).
Replies may be 64000 tokens. A reply cut off tells you whether you were thinking or writing; a second cut-off ends the goal.
T.light(box, opts) gives block light over the map; T.darkCells(box, opts) the cells where mobs could spawn: opts.dimension = "nether" groups them (7 or less: skeletons, wither skeletons, endermen; 11 or less: zombified piglins, piglins, hoglins, blazes; ghasts and magma cubes whatever the light). Your helpers/lightmodel.lua can go.
T.attachedTo(x, y, z) lists what hangs on or stands on a block (torches, dust, buttons, ladders, signs, rails...); T.digCell(x, y, z, {attached = "refuse"}) won't drop them.
T.placeAt(x, y, z, sign, {text = ..., toward = {x, y, z}}) faces a sign at a reader (a wall sign if a block stands behind it, else standing, the nearest of 4 rotations); T.signFor(reader, text) picks the spot in front of them.
Torches and standing signs go on top of fences, walls and panes. Redstone dust, string, seeds and crops, vines and the wall forms of signs, banners and heads are placed by their item (T.itemOfBlock, T.blockOfItem); a plan may say redstone_wire. Pistons, dispensers, droppers, observers, repeaters and comparators are placed by facing.
opts.digBelow: a floor that drops itself needs only a free slot; on solid ground it digs a neighbouring floor cell as a way out and puts both back; every need is named at once.
T.look notes what it sees in the map, so a block placeAt or T.build finds already there no longer reads as air to T.lint.
T.lint of a plan tags warnings "plan" or "around", and T.build's player: lines show the plan's own; a lone stair bench is no flight; a new "light" rule warns of a wall torch in a walkway.
A route that fails because of furnishings says "a way exists through minecraft:wall_torch at x,y,z (lift it and put it back)". Nether portals are never planned through.
T.cfg.tools = {"*_pickaxe"} (names or globs) makes only the equipped and listed tools essential, so T.stow puts looted swords and axes away. Put it in your settings helper.
T.addChest(x, y, z, side, bulk, {holds = {"*_log", ...}}) gives a chest a category; T.stow prefers it; T.sortChests() moves misplaced stacks. Your helpers/storage.lua can go. T.stow's info.reasons says why each item stayed; T.recount(side) re-reads a chest; crafting at a known chest re-reads it.
Built-in recipes: barrel, redstone torch, repeater, lever, stone button, iron door, respawn anchor, soul campfire. Your helpers/recipes_extra.lua and recipes_redstone.lua can go.
Already built in, so these helpers can go: say and askPlayer (the chat tool, T.report, T.report.ask), batchCraft (T.craftBatch), digCell (T.digCell), furnaceSmelt (T.smeltAt). T.harvest takes opts.maxFuel and opts.timeout.
T.undo now puts back redstone dust and planted crops too.
The log says when the cache was written again and why, and when a reply was cut off.
-- ADR-0013 (#41-#51, #87-#89): compaction, skills, chat while code runs, reports to everyone, the light model, attachments, signs toward a reader, digBelow's exit, chest categories, T.cfg.tools, the lint split, item names.

== 0.52.0
T.lint(box or plan) lists what stops a two-tall player: under 3 air over a stair, a flight's approach or exit blocked, railings meeting only on a diagonal, unrailed drops over 3, a lantern at head height. Warnings only.
T.build now ends with "player: ..." lines from T.lint (its result is unchanged; player = false turns it off); T.verify(plan, {player = true}) adds info.warnings.
T.walkable(from, to) says whether the player can walk from one feet cell to another (steps up 1, stairs, slabs, ladders; falls over 3 listed), with the path or why not.
T.placeAt places signs (on a wall, or standing) and writes opts.text, wrapped to 4 lines of 15; T.placeDir(d, text) does too.
T.placeAt places ladders from above and from below, turning to face the wall they hang on.
A top slab under a stair is now built after the stair; a blueprint legend entry may say "after dx,dy,dz" to order its cells.
A top half with nothing above it gets a temporary block (cobblestone, dirt, planks, bricks, or opts.temp) placed above, then the item from below, then the block taken away.
opts.digBelow = true (placeAt and T.build) does your dig-the-floor trick: dig, stand there, place up, put the same floor block back. Carry one of the floor block. Without it the floor is never dug.
Routes with dig = true no longer dig ladders, trapdoors, fence gates, signs, lanterns, torches or rails: they go round, and "it met" names them.
A route to a walled-in goal fails at once with "walled in by ...": open the way or use opts.inside; a bigger limit won't help.
harvest's "no free cell beside it" now says opts.move = {dig = true} tunnels in.
mine.job, an unfinished T.build and the undo journal record the frame they were made in; after a re-zero they refuse to go on and are marked stale, and T.build(plan) starts afresh. T.frameId() and T.frameStale(stamp) are new.
What the player says in chat while you think now comes with your next results ("<name> said in chat: ... (at t)"): a chat box aboard goes on the free side while the request runs, and the pickaxe is back before your code runs. The chunk controller never moves. Chat while your code runs is still missed.
While a goal runs, /claude/resume.txt holds it (its first line is the session's options: keep it). Add where you are and what's left as you go. If the player has turned on resuming, a reboot brings you back with "You were interrupted (reboot)" and that goal.
Keep the T.keep items, T.cfg values and T.diggable every session should have in a keep() helper: a session starts with turtlelib's defaults.
claude.log now keeps 3 old files (claude.log.1 to .3), fewer when the disk is short.
-- ADR-0012 (#33-#36): the player-access lint, placing with support, the chat listener, resume, log keeping.

== 0.51.0
Blocks of nine craft both ways with no recipe added (coal and coal blocks, bone meal and bone blocks, iron, gold, nuggets...): the T.addRecipe calls in your helpers.lua can go.
T.craftBatch(list) crafts many recipes with one stash and leaves the products in the chest (opts.take brings them aboard): your batchCraft can go.
T.craftPlan{[name] = n} works out the steps down to logs, the base materials and what's short; then T.craftBatch(plan).
T.smeltAt({x, y, z}, item, n) smelts at a furnace you placed ({wait = false} returns at once; n = 0 collects later): your furnaceSmelt can go.
T.fetch(name, n, {stay = true}) skips the walk back; T.inventoryReport() totals everything with each count's age; T.prepare{need = {...}, freeSlots = k} stows, fetches and checks in one trip.
T.build(plan, {order = "column"}) builds walls column by column for less fuel; a build stops with "circling: ..." when its fuel runs over 3 times the estimate (fuelWatch = false turns that off).
Route digs (moveTo/travel with dig=) refuse a cell with lava or a water source beside or above it: "digging X at c would release lava at c'". Pass liquid = true to dig anyway; T.liquidNear(x,y,z) tells you first. A dig under gravel returns a warning as the second result.
T.digCell(x,y,z) digs exactly one cell from beside it, refusing liquid or protected cells: your helpers.lua digCell can go. moveTo{digOnly={"x,y,z",...}} limits a route's digs to those cells.
Routes cross protected areas through air the map knows (doorways, halls); they never dig there. Don't raise limit for a "no route" through an area: read what it says walled it in.
T.inside({"keep","wall"}, fn) owns several areas, so a cell in two overlapping areas can be dug; nested T.inside calls add up. T.withUnprotected is the same.
T.trail() lists the last 200 cells you moved through; T.retrace(n) walks back n of them, skipping loops: your helpers.lua retrace can go.
The map no longer forgets cubes in protected areas or round home; T.map.pin(box) keeps other places; T.map.stats().over says when it can't shrink further.
T.harvest(..., {maxFuel=, timeout=, exclude={...}}) caps a dig job (info.spent is the fuel used); T.fuelFor counts scans of radius 8 or less as free.
The chat tool says something in the game's chat, or with ask = true asks there and waits for an answer from the chat or the keyboard; T.report.ask(question, opts) does it from code: replace your askPlayer with them.
You are reminded when 15 minutes pass without a word to the player in chat (the setting claude.remindChat).
keep(name, code) runs a helper and keeps it in /claude/helpers/<name>.lua, one file a helper, each loaded on its own: move what /claude/helpers.lua holds there, a helper at a time.
A call whose code makes a function local at its top level gets a note that it is gone with the call.
Each lua result ends with a status line: where the turtle is, its fuel and what the call used, free slots, and lava or water beside it.
Old tool calls' code and old thinking are cleared from the conversation too; the person can type /new for a new conversation (memory and files carry over).
T.cfg.maxHeight is 256 in a session (it was 16).
A lua call with review = true shows its code to the person at the turtle and runs only on a yes.
T.up, T.down, T.forward and T.back now return false and why ("maxHeight 16 reached", "blocked by X at x,y,z", "no fuel"): read the why.
T.keep, T.diggable and moveTo's dig and avoid take a list or a set of names, and globs ("*netherrack") wherever names are matched.
A finished job (its last step done) no longer rides on T.report's messages; T.job.clear() ends it.
T.cfg.autoDump tidies first, says when a trip fails, and turns itself off after two failures in a row; T.dumpJunk finds its way out of an enclosure.
T.refuelFromLava leaves lava in protected areas alone (opts.exclude names more).
The chest catalogue keeps one record per chest (a double chest was counted twice); T.where says how old each count is.
T.placeAt's failure says which approaches it tried and what each lacked.
T.inspectAt goes round what is in the way by default.
"no route" names the protected areas and doors that walled the search in: a bigger limit may not help.
T.fetch says its count once; T.smelt takes back its own furnace inside a protected area; T.setOrigin warns of saved jobs in the old frame.
print, write, io.write, printError, setmetatable, getmetatable, rawget, rawset, xpcall, keys and colours work in your code, and a print at the top of a helper file no longer breaks it.
